Choose the variables

Variables used to find clusters define what "similar patients" means: choose those measured before any treatment and relevant to the question. Keep outcomes as describe clusters only: they then show whether the clusters differ in outcome without having shaped them.

Check the data (optional)

Looks for likely data-entry errors in the variables you use, and for unusual patients among the clustering variables, before the analysis. Nothing changes unless you choose to leave a patient out.

Choose the approach

Filling with the median is quick and fine when few values are missing. Multiple imputation (chained equations with predictive mean matching) fills each missing value from patients with similar recorded values, five times over, clusters each version and combines them; it also shows whether the clusters depend on the imputed values. Only the clustering variables are used to impute, so outcomes cannot shape the clusters. Patients missing every clustering variable are best removed first.
